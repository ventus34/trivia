# Audit: Turning Points in History (`turning_points_in_history_en`)

Reviewed the description and all 100 question records, including questions, options, answer keys and both explanations. Changed 56 records: **1, 3, 5, 7, 9–11, 16, 18, 20–21, 23, 25, 28–33, 36–37, 40–42, 44–45, 47–48, 52–53, 56–57, 59, 62–63, 65, 68–70, 73–74, 76–78, 81–82, 85, 87, 89, 92–94, 96, 98–100**. Numbers refer to one-based question positions. The pack retains 100 questions and its existing description; the index was not changed. Earlier automated verification metadata was removed from changed records and the pack level.

- Corrected or qualified historical claims about Gutenberg and earlier movable type (25), the Emancipation Proclamation's limited scope (37), Henry Dunant and the ICRC's five founders (44), and the Curie team's discoveries (47).
- Corrected the claim that the 1908 Model T was the first car made on an assembly line: Ford's moving line was introduced in 1913, while Oldsmobile had earlier assembly methods (48). Distinguished the 1918 armistice from the 1919 Treaty of Versailles (78), and Maastricht's entry into force in 1993 from the euro's launch in 1999 (76).
- Removed ambiguous competing answers and answers embedded in questions, including the Cuban missile crisis (69), Genghis Khan's personal name (73), the Gran Colombia presidency (53) and pasteurization (56). Replaced a subjective ranking of early web portals with the documented founders of Yahoo (92).
- Qualified disputed or oversimplified claims about Narmer (20), Luther's posting of the theses (18), the reported second Gulf of Tonkin attack (42), Marie Antoinette's alleged quotation (74), and causes of Japan's surrender (41). Acknowledged Indigenous allies in the conquest of Tenochtitlan and the Lewis and Clark expedition (33, 93).
