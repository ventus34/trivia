## Limitations

The entire pack received editorial review and targeted source checks; not every factual assertion was independently verified. The original category mostly asks general technology questions, with many items lacking a specific fictional work, date or demonstrated influence. Consequently it should not be interpreted as establishing that each invention was uniquely predicted by science fiction or caused by a particular work. Some technologies are experimental, vary by product or change over time. A fuller rewrite with sourced work-to-technology comparisons would be a separate editorial project.

## Follow-up: work-to-technology comparisons

Re-read all 100 questions, options and explanations and checked selected fictional comparisons against the sources below. Reworked questions 20, 30, 34, 38, 42 and 48 to name a fictional vessel or work and relate its features to a real technology. The revised questions distinguish resemblance from documented influence: Verne did not invent the submarine, a smart speaker is not the fully capable Star Trek computer, virtual reality does not reproduce the physical holodeck, and 3D printing is less versatile than a replicator. The wrist-radio question cites Dick Tracy's 1946 comic-strip device without attributing all smartwatches to it. Removed outdated verification metadata from the changed records.

Sources for this follow-up: [Library of Congress — Nemo and the fictional Nautilus](https://www.loc.gov/item/2004671865/); [NASA interview — Star Trek displays and communicators](https://www.nasa.gov/podcasts/invisible-network/bonus-dan-curry-nasas-the-invisible-network-podcast/); [NASA discussion of Star Trek computer and voice assistants](https://www.nasa.gov/podcasts/houston-we-have-a-podcast/nasas-60th-anniversary-live/); [Smithsonian — Dick Tracy's two-way wrist radio](https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_892847); [NASA — virtual reality and the holodeck](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19950010483/downloads/19950010483.pdf); [NASA — 3D printing and the replicator](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/nasa-asme-star-trek-challenge-future-engineers-to-turn-science-fiction-into-science-fact/).

**Remaining work:** Most other records remain generic technology questions without an identifiable fictional work or independently sourced relationship to it. The pack still needs a larger, source-based rewrite to meet its indexed title; completing this follow-up does not resolve that limitation. Source checks covered selected comparisons, not every factual assertion in the pack.

## Follow-up: specific works and real-world comparisons

Reworked 11 further records (8, 12, 21, 36, 40, 53, 60, 63, 68, 73 and 91), replacing generic technology definitions with identifiable fictional works or documented connections to real technology. Examples include Verne's lunar projectile versus Apollo's Saturn V, the Star Wars training droid and NASA's SPHERES research robots, The Jetsons and video calls or robot vacuums, Gibson's cyberspace and brain-computer interfaces, Bradbury's in-ear radios and modern earbuds, and Iron Man alongside NASA's X1 exoskeleton. Where the source supports resemblance rather than a causal connection, the explanation says so; X1 and present-day brain interfaces do not reproduce the fictional capabilities. The Enterprise question explicitly identifies it as an atmospheric test vehicle rather than an orbital spacecraft.

Sources: [NASA — Verne's cannon and lunar orbit](https://www.nasa.gov/history/Apollobib/ch6.html); [NASA — Apollo and Saturn V compared with Verne](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19670002917/downloads/19670002917.pdf); [NASA — SPHERES and Star Wars](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/nasa-spheres-these-are-droids-youre-looking/); [Smithsonian — StarTAC, The Jetsons and real devices](https://airandspace.si.edu/learn/programs/soar-together/sci-fi-modern-tech); [NASA — Enterprise naming and flight tests](https://www.nasa.gov/history/40-years-ago-space-shuttle-enterprise-rolls-to-the-pad/); [MIT — Neuromancer, Snow Crash and early avatar spaces](https://alumni.media.mit.edu/~hannes/msthesis/1_introduction.html); [Bradbury, Fahrenheit 451 — text](https://web.english.upenn.edu/~cavitch/pdf-library/Bradbury_Fahrenheit_451.pdf); [NASA — X1 exoskeleton](https://www.nasa.gov/technology/nasas-ironman-like-exoskeleton-could-give-astronauts-paraplegics-improved-mobility-and-strength/).

**Remaining work:** Many other questions still define everyday technologies without identifying a fictional work or substantiating a connection. The present additions reduce that gap but do not constitute a complete thematic rewrite or independent verification of all facts. Existing explanations and options outside the changed records were not comprehensively reviewed in this follow-up.
